#2.c) Explaining significance of E-value and why we use a threshold
# The E value (Expected value) estimates how likely it is your match occurred by chance, with a low E value thus meaning your match is statistically significant (less likely to have occurred by chance) and a high value meaning it is not statistically significant. A threshold removes matches that are likely random and not statistically significant, therefore allowing us to focus on only important matches. This is important when there are hundreds or thousands of matches.

#5.a) Difference between pairwise alignment and profile-based searching
#Pairwise alignment compares a pair of DNA/RNA/protein sequences and is limited to only a pair. It compares them to find similar/different regions and uses a consistent scoring system at every position.
#Profile-based searching, in our case, uses a Hidden Markov Model (HMM), which is built from a multiple sequence alignment (MSA). A specific sequence is then compared to the HMM where conserverd positions count heavily and variable lightly. 
#Pairwise alignment works best with similar sequences and sequences with no known family. The profile-based searching would be better for genes and proteins that are much different, potentially due to evolution.   

#5.b)  Why is building an HMM profile from multiple sequences more sensitive than using a single sequence as a BLAST query?
#An HHM profile is more sensitive because it uses position specific data to weight the matches. In particular, after an 
#MSA is built, regions of similiarty are put into a column. It then evaluates the per column probability and 
#evaluates these and punishes differences at highly conserved sites and not as much for variable sites. This allows 
#it to detect sections which have diverged so far that BLAST cant detect them. This is because BLAST applies the
#query to a BLOSUM62 (susbtitution matrix) that is applied identically to each postion.
